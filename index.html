CH. 0129 OCT 2025

Two Guys and a Drill Joke

TiT was born at 18:00 on October 29, 2025, when Flip Co and Bane Inc. sat alone in an empty guild chat. Four hours later a third voice showed up.

the d stands for drill
or daveBig Ds Mining Inc.

That is, verbatim, the second joke ever told in this guild's history, and it set the tone for the entire year that followed. Within minutes the three of them were dreaming absurdly big for a guild that didn't yet have a name worth remembering — guild-only exchanges, "guild space portals," guild warp drives, a guild HQ that doubles as its own economy. None of it existed yet. They had, between them, one smelter.

By the next morning they'd settled into specialties almost by accident — Flip into food, Bane into construction, Big D into raw ore and smelting — and that instinct, everyone picks a lane and we trade the gaps, became the guild's entire economic philosophy for the next twelve months.

CH. 02NOV 2025

The First Wave

Through November 2025 the guild filled out with its founding generation: Dublina Corps (Ireland), MB Industries (a Dane who'd fled to Norway), Astella Interplanetary Trading, Rumball Processors. They were scattered across timezones badly enough that Astella once greeted the room with "Good morning to Flip, Good day Dublina, Its tomorrow for Big D, and I'm enjoying my evening" — a guild that never really slept, from day one.

Growth was almost accidental. Nobody understood the donation or prestige systems, HQ upgrades got funded by whoever happened to have spare glass that week, and by November 8 they'd cracked the top 30 guilds (rank #29) and Astella was already crowing "mazel tov."

Then, on November 23, the guild's first real economic mind arrived: Galactic Metallurgist Union — GMU — a metallurgist who treated the game like an actual industry. GMU's philosophy ("establishing a firm, healthy foundation before venturing into exotic metals") shaped the guild's early culture of patient, honest production, and GMU would spend the next two months mentoring newcomers, updating the guild board, and single-handedly explaining steel-market cycles to anyone who'd listen.

CH. 0321 DEC 2025

The First Kick

TiT's first real drama landed on December 21, 2025, when GMU unilaterally kicked a member called MoleRatGas for being, essentially, unable to stop LARPing.

I asked him what his plan was. He said he wanted to make titanium… I don't want a guy like that in our guildGMU

Flip backed the call ("Happy to see him go personally… he just replies with dumb stuff"), and the guild's unwritten constitution got its first clause: be real, or don't bother.

CH. 04DEC 2025 – JAN 2026

The Chicken Mafia Begins

Guild character accelerated fast once Drunkenduo's Ruthless Dividend — "DrD," Dutch, 42-going-on-43, allergic to being taken seriously — joined on December 19. Within a month DrD had:

  • adopted a pet chicken named Clucky, later "fired as General Manager of DrD" for mishandling a market crisis, then reassigned to HR;
  • launched the $500 Truss Club, a genuine, functioning price-fixing cartel on the :truss market, built entirely through what DrD called "diplomacy" (bullying other guilds' top producers into an honor system);
  • renamed a planet Truss Me Bro, then later Welcome At Weldy's;
  • and declared, with a straight face, "I'm not your pal, friend" to a man who refused to sell him a lobster.

GMU, ever the principled one, publicly refused to join:

The GMU is dedicated to competitive and fair rates on goods and has no interest in shady dealings. No offense.GMU

The cartel ran anyway, for weeks, crashing and reforming around coffee, vitaqua, and reinforced glass in turn. By mid-January 2026 the guild had added B0dega (Seattle, electronics), Yzaxtol Drillers, Karsian Industries, Sirius Corp, and Skunk Industries — and a mascot problem: Clucky had an entire abduction storyline, zalgo-text tributes, and her own catchphrase ("think big").

CH. 05JAN 2026

Losing the Founders

Arcturus Initiative — a chatty, broke-by-choice member who'd negotiated a donation exemption directly with Flip — had, by his own admission, "absolutely never wanting to donate," and by January 11 he was gone. DrD's eulogy: "I kind of liked him though… Made for fun conversations."

Worse, on January 21, 2026, GMU — the guild's economic conscience from day one — announced he was quitting the game outright.

Been an honor playing with you guys. I am leaving the game. It takes too much of my time now. I have grown as much as I'd like to grow.GMU

It's one of the few completely sincere, un-joking lines in the entire chat log, and the guild treated it that way.

CH. 06EARLY–MID 2026

Climbing the Board

What GMU left behind kept compounding. The guild's rank climbed almost monthly — #45 at founding, #29, #25, #21, #16, #12, #8, #7 — fueled by relentless HQ-upgrade donation pushes ("PRIORITY 1: HQ"), new recruits (Cucklor Logistics — "Cracklor," a New Zealand construction specialist who got meme-renamed by the guild's own joke-bot after a $200 bet, Caboose Inc., Technically Federated Reserves, Romero Enterprises, fresh off defecting from a guild literally called the Ferengi Alliance), and increasingly coordinated market operations — composite-truss walls, coffee corners, fission-fuel monopolies — openly discussed in chat with the casual menace of a cartel that has never once been punished for it.

CH. 0728 APR 2026

Self-Mythologizing

The clearest proof TiT had become a culture, not just a spreadsheet, arrived on April 28, 2026, when ZugZug fed the day's chat into an AI and published The TiT Guild Character Dossier straight into guild chat — a gleeful, half-affectionate roast of its own members, including:

  • The Jobb Officer Crisis — Jobb made officer as a joke, DrD protest-quit the guild over it, Flip discovered he lacked the permissions to un-officer anyone, and Jobb voluntarily demoted himself. Total runtime: twenty minutes.
  • Orion Constructs, the Brief Visit — recruited, vetted with a chicken-themed trick question, and booted the next day for being "too smol."
  • The KOI Water War — a full afternoon of improvised economic warfare against a suspected bot, with "concrete results unclear" and "energy extremely high."
  • The Great DrD Pandemic — half the guild briefly renamed itself DrD in tribute, until Cracklor "disowned" the first defector.

This is, functionally, the guild writing its own oral history in real time — which is also, in its way, exactly what this page is doing now.

CH. 082026, ONGOING

Leadership as Performance Art

Nobody in TiT ever really ran anything after Flip's early days. Leadership became a running bit: Cracklor was "so-called leader," then unofficially "gone," leaving "DrD in charge" with, as one member put it, "no rules." Then Pear won a popularity vote nobody fully understood ("Pear won the vote? … But pear somehow is still leader? Idk how it works."). Then MantiMatter got crowned "another leader we didn't ask for." By May 2026 a new member asked point blank why 40% of the guild held officer rank, and Pear's answer was the whole guild's governing philosophy in five words:

we have a flat leadership modelPear Inc.
CH. 09OCT 2026

The Long Grind, and the Ending

The back half of 2026 was the guild at scale — billions in daily income, antimatter reactors, guild-built tools (Flip's Chrome extension, DrD's titguild.com, which earned him the title "vibe code god," MantiMatter's own extension) — and a slow creeping exhaustion that shows up again and again by October: Vegetable Goblin (the artist formerly, and still sometimes, known as Farmy McFarm Face The 4th) repeatedly threatening to quit, ASCII-art despair, and burnout jokes that stopped being entirely jokes.

And then, in the chat's final days, the real twist: Flip Co — guild founder, food baron, year-long "supreme leader" — revealed he'd been quietly building the game's successor, a new project called O&O, with TiT as his alpha testers.

8 days until I disband TiT. Erm I mean O&O launches.Flip Co

DrD, asked why anyone was still here after the game itself had stopped being fun months ago, gave the only answer that actually explains 289,000 messages and a full year of chicken-based economic terrorism:

game sucks but we're still here for the social element. That has been the last 8 months for me and I learned to live with it.DrD

The last line in the entire chat log, fittingly, isn't a joke or a trade or a chicken. It's someone quietly archiving the whole thing: "Download the chat history… Guild Trade & Infrastructure Tech… Loading… 11,656 messages… this is gonna take a while." It did. This page is what came out the other side.

CH. 0106 OCT 2025

Two Strangers, One Spreadsheet Problem

F4T started a few weeks before TiT did, and it shows. At 20:42 on October 6, 2025, Zdeyland Chutani (Poland, agriculture) and Demdigs (iron and steel, frustrated that his first planet "only has iron ore and o2") found each other in an empty guild chat and started doing something almost nobody does on day one of a game: patiently explaining the economy to each other, line by line. Delivery missions, research-cost inflation, fertility bonuses, where to find hydrogen. "Good to know," Demdigs says, over and over, like a man taking notes. By the end of the first night Zdeyland signs off with "going to bed, cya." Demdigs answers "good night." It is, structurally, the calmest possible way to found a guild.

Within days a third regular, Dan Co. (Australia), and then Arconia Brewery (Switzerland) joined the same unhurried tutorial. The guild tag got fixed by the developers after Zdeyland asked on Discord, and by October 8 they were "top 7 guild (up from top 9)" — the first of what would become a defining trait: F4T didn't claw up from the bottom the way some guilds did. It arrived near the top early and mostly stayed there, trading the #1 through #6 spots with a small set of rivals for the next year.

CH. 02OCT 2025, ONGOING

Recruiting on Talkativeness Alone

The guild's second member policy, set within its first week, never really changed: when two candidates apply and you can only take one, take the one who talks in chat. S.T.A.R Labs got in that way on October 10 ("he'll have a surprise once he's back then :p"), and immediately justified it by narrating the guild's rank climb play-by-play. GIAG got in partly because Dan Co had seen him post. The unspoken rule, stated outright a few months later by Zdeyland:

if you'll not log in for few days, we may kick you to give room to someone active… that's basically the only rule we have hereZdeyland Chutani

F4T, in other words, optimized ruthlessly for a chat that stayed alive — and the chat log is 57,621 real messages long, so it worked.

CH. 03EARLY–MID 2026

The Traitor List

Every guild gets a myth. TiT got a chicken. F4T got a ledger. Sometime before February 2026, S.T.A.R Labs started keeping what the chat calls, completely straight-faced, the Traitor List — a running roll of anyone who leaves the guild badly. The rules, as STAR eventually codified them out loud: a single goodbye sentence before vanishing gets you on it; a heartfelt paragraph thanking everyone gets you off the hook. When [ATS] Vamp left in June, he wrote four full sentences explaining his reasoning and donated extra "as a small thank-you" specifically, he said, so his "name won't be in the traitor list." It worked. Berlin food distributions inherited the list later in the year and kept it updated like a civic duty: "our history is growing as well."

STAR didn't stop there. On May 26, 2026, unprompted, they posted "The Officially Unofficial History of Food For Thoughts" straight into chat — dated entries chronicling the guild's own past, including the single best line in the whole log:

02/03/2026 S.C.A.M INTERSTELLAR left the guild due to Jobb dissing British cuisineThe Officially Unofficial History of Food For Thoughts

(Jobb, infamous elsewhere for a twenty-minute officer crisis in TiT, apparently also torched a diplomatic incident over cuisine here.) S.C.A.M INTERSTELLAR renamed itself twice and eventually settled on the handle "scam" — who eighteen months of chat later is still around, defending vitaqua stockpiles and getting accused of running something illegal on the exchange.

CH. 04SEP 2026

The Cosspocalypse

Not every legend was a member. Coss, a market whale other guilds talked about in awed, nervous tones for most of 2026 ("the sheer quantities of iron Coss produces," "Coss manipulated that," "Coss is starting a war with Iran" over antimatter supply), finally broke in September:

I'm dismantling all of my bases, I've lost almost 10B Cv already, I am quitting… I've done my time, there's not enough new things to do… I will settle on the rim with a base and live out my days thereCoss

F4T watched the fallout from the sidelines, selling into the chaos ("Coss dumping goods as he departs") and coining the aftermath "the Cosspocalypse" without irony.

CH. 05OCT 2026

A Guild That Noticed It Was Hollowing Out

What sets F4T's back half apart from most trade-guild chatter is that its members diagnosed their own burnout out loud, calmly, like it was just another production chain to optimize. By October 2026, Zdeyland was telling the room exactly why the fun had curdled:

there's simply no endgame… when you already have 20 bases then its not fun anymore, its tedious. So you build more warehouses and bigger ships, just to play lessZdeyland Chutani

Shermy's Canteen admitted to thinking about quitting "3 or 4 times a week." The departures piled up across the whole game that autumn — Capita, Sweeper Conglomerate, Dakarlocat, half a dozen others — not kicked, just gone, often announced in a single apologetic line that landed them on the Traitor List whether they meant to or not.

Leadership drifted the same low-key way. On October 10, with STAR half-joking about defecting to TiT "for a day or so" because "game is getting boring," the leader role got handed to Berlin food distributions — then asked back two days later ("Berlin gimme leader role back… pwetty pwease"), returned without ceremony, no vote, no crisis, just two people trading a title between trades.

CH. 0630 OCT 2026

The Guild That Got Caught Being Archived

F4T was never isolated. Its chat is threaded with TiT names the whole way through — Pass 4 Denied, Grr, meowco, Murderous Trading Corps — guildmates, rivals, and occasional defectors drifting between the two, close enough that Nestlé Galactic Corporation could joke "general chat is TiT chat, if you spend long enough in there you get an invite." Astro Construction Enterprise left F4T in February entirely because TiT's members "kept talking about Chickens" and curiosity won.

And on the log's very last day, at 15:19 on October 30, 2026 — minutes after Unicorn Industries noted that meowco had defected to TiT "perminently" and gone onto the Traitor List anyway — a new voice drops into F4T chat: Vegetable Goblin, a TiT regular, passing through.

Doing the history of F4T through the guild chat… Already done history of TiT… and wall of membersVegetable Goblin

Zdeyland, founder, still here thirteen months later, asks the only reasonable question left: "are we a bus stop or what." That's the actual last exchange in the file. This page is the history Vegetable Goblin said, right there in the chat, that they were going to write.

CH. 0103 OCT 2025

Two Spreadsheets Meet

GP started four days before even TiT or F4T existed. At 15:09 on October 3, 2025, a player called Resource Extraction Services found Space Inc. alone in a guild chat with no name worth remembering yet, and the first thing either of them did was talk logistics: Administration Centers, overhead percentages, hull plates. By October 4 a third voice, Megafood Inc., had wandered in ("Cheers pioneers! 🍻"), and within a week Space Inc. was publicly sharing a Google Sheet cataloguing every planet, resource, and building in the game, captioned with the driest possible instruction: "please only edit yellow cells." That spreadsheet, more than any slogan, is GP's founding document.

What's strange, in hindsight, is that two of the four people who built GP's early culture — Resource Extraction Services and Megafood — never actually joined the guild. They are tagged as guests in every single log line. RES sent 45 messages over five months of patient advice and then quietly stopped showing up. Megafood ran the guild's entire food supply chain by hand for months, apologizing when he was slow, updating prices daily, never once wearing the [GP] tag. GP's economy, in other words, was partly built by people who were never members of it.

CH. 02OCT 2025, ONGOING

The Pioneer Economy

By October 8 the guild had cracked its tag with the developers and started filling out: TwilightTaste (agriculture, still here thirteen months later), Valdoria (chemistry and electronics), Intergalactic Fruit Company. The culture that set in immediately was almost aggressively collaborative — nobody undercut a guildmate for sport, everyone posted "do you need X" before hauling to the exchange, and Space Inc. personally funded most of the early HQ and Administration Center levels out of his own production. When he tested whether trading an item back and forth could inflate its donation value, he reported the exploit straight to the developer he knew personally from a different game, on the record, in guild chat:

I do can sell it on the market for the highest price and see if someone wants to buy it for that price. I can't ask someone to buy it.Space Inc.

It is, structurally, the least cartel-like thing anyone in any of these three guilds ever did. The guild had its own benchmark for ambition from the start, and he was never a member: a player called ACME, who hit four bases while everyone else was still deciding on a second. "That ACME guy is already at 4 bases :D," Space Inc. marveled on October 9, and for over a year ACME stayed the guild's informal yardstick — a supplier of uranium ore, a rival in food rankings, a cautionary tale about burnout once he finally slowed down for Christmas.

CH. 03DEC 2025

The Week the Markets Broke

GP's one real brush with outside drama arrived at Christmas 2025, and it wasn't their own. A cluster of players from a rival guild — "lek," "lighting," "garco" — had reportedly been manipulating the copper market to inflate their extraction rankings; the developers caught it, reverted it, and the players quit en masse. GP watched the fallout from the sidelines as entire markets emptied overnight: "Emptied the :Advanced research data market, garco leaving left some big hole I guess." TwilightTaste, half-joking, demanded the developers add automation or GP would leave too. Nobody believed him.

He will never quitSpace Inc.'s own members, about TwilightTaste

Correctly — Space Inc. was still posting in the exact same chat, about the exact same markets, on October 30, 2026, the log's last day.

CH. 042026, ONGOING

A Guild That Climbs Quietly

There was never a leadership crisis in GP. No officer got kicked as a joke, no cartel needed a straight-faced constitution, no popularity vote anyone had to explain. Space Inc. simply kept building, kept answering questions, kept absorbing whatever anyone's polymer plant produced ("Try showing 500 :Battery to Space Inc. They will be in his pocket before you can blink," one chemist marveled in December), and the guild's rank climbed the unglamorous way: medal by medal, category by category. "We're doing our best to keep rank 1," TwilightTaste announced on New Year's Day 2026, and for stretches of the year that was simply true, in food, in construction, in metals, traded back and forth with the same two or three rival outfits.

New members arrived less as recruits and more as specialists slotting into gaps: Skippy, Medici Mining Conglomerate, KACT, a player named Folkersen Inc. who by late 2026 was running an actual automated bot that posted scheduled "AI Market Watch" supply reports straight into chat. The guild's international roster, revealed almost by accident one December night (Belgian, British, French, Spanish, Slovak, Canadian, in that order, around a joke about Dutch bitterballen), never seemed to slow any of it down.

CH. 052026

The Guild's Own Running Joke

If TiT had a chicken and F4T had a Traitor List, GP had a player who announced he was quitting so many times across 2026 that it became a genuine guild pastime to ask whether he meant it. "Did he quit again? Or do bases not show when in vacation mode," Space Inc. asked on the log's very last day. "It looks like he's really leaving this time," KACT replied, then immediately undercut the sincerity of it:

all the previous times he really, really left and sold his assets to donate to the guild, he ended up donating nothing and started breaking the marketsKACT

It is the closest thing GP has to a legend, and it is, fittingly, a legend about someone who never actually leaves.

CH. 06OCT 2026

The Tourists

GP was never as isolated as its calm chat suggests. A GP member named KACT turns up briefly in TiT's own logs. In the other direction, on October 9, 2026, TiT's own leader — Pear Inc., the guild that famously runs on "a flat leadership model" — wandered into GP's chat as a guest, bantering about leaderboard rankings with GP's EnderKenobi1 ("Ender - stay no4 ok k thx ❤️") and doing admin-center math out loud before signing off the next day with a crooked little "0/ GP." GP's Medici Mining Conglomerate even mentioned, unprompted, that Coss — the market whale F4T watched implode in September — had been "kicked from ATS discord and guild" for economic griefing and "moved to TiT." The three guilds were never really three separate stories. They were one universe, sorted into three chat windows.

CH. 0730 OCT 2026

The Ending, Again

The very last entries in GP's log, on October 30, 2026, are a new arrival announcing themselves: Vegetable Goblin, the same TiT regular who had already written the history of TiT and the history of F4T, dropping into GP's chat with the same line: "starting now, will let you know when done." Medici Mining Conglomerate welcomed them with one word — "Preserving history" — and a GP member named aldera mining company placed them immediately, correctly, by their TiT reputation.

it is the vegetable goblin that sprouted out of farmys corpsealdera mining company

CH. 0123 SEP 2025

Player #52

General starts five days before any guild does, in a chat with maybe a dozen people in it. "Hey everyone!" opens a user called Leki Fuel and Stuff, tagged [DEV] — one of the two developers who will spend the next year answering tutorial questions in character, as a fellow tycoon. On September 28, 2025, a new arrival called Dhitauys7 asks how many people are even playing. Resource Extraction Services — not yet an F4T institution, just a helpful stranger — answers straight: "I believe you are player #52. The game has only been going for 4 days now." Space Inc. (not yet GP's founder, just a guy who likes mornings) says good morning to the galaxy and means it literally, because the galaxy is small enough to greet.

It would not stay small. By October 8 someone's predicting the server will hit 1,000 players "tmr." By November 19 a player has scrolled to the bottom of the rankings and counted roughly 4,500. By January 2026 Spegepølse is estimating total registered players north of 4,800 just from someone's rank number. General chat is the one place in this whole archive where you can watch a brand-new multiplayer economy go from fifty-two strangers saying hello to a server nobody can fully see the edges of anymore, in real time, in public.

CH. 0201–12 OCT 2025

The Federal Bureau of Economic Excellence

The two developers never break character. Leki Fuel and Stuff runs chemistry; Builder Inc. runs construction; both answer rules questions as if they, too, were just another company trying to make a living. On October 1, 2025, Leki posts the single most elaborate piece of writing either of them will ever produce in General: a full in-universe awards ceremony, GALACTIC FEDERATION HONORS — SEPTEMBER 2025, gold-medal emoji and all, crediting Megafood Inc. and TwilightTaste and Space Inc. by name for growth and production records, signed "Federal Bureau of Economic Excellence." It is a lovely piece of worldbuilding. It also never happens again. By October 2026, patch notes from the same developer read: "Update #32: See #WhatsNew" and "Not yet" — ticket numbers standing in for sentences. The game got too big for ceremony. The devs didn't stop caring; they just ran out of room to be whimsical about it, which is its own quiet data point about what running this galaxy for thirteen months actually cost them.

Twelve days after the honors ceremony, on October 12, the funniest bug report of the whole log lands, and the devs fix it with a straight face: eggs required Research Tier 4 and chickens required Tier 5, so the galaxy had invented a way to produce eggs before it was possible to own the chicken that laid them. Leki's patch note is titled, correctly, "The Great Chicken-Egg Paradox." This happens in General chat in mid-October 2025 — more than two months before a Dutch cartel boss named DrD will join a guild called TiT and adopt a pet chicken as its entire personality. The chicken was never his bit. The chicken was a server-wide joke before TiT even had a face to put on it; DrD just had the good sense to notice a punchline lying in the street and pick it up.

CH. 03DEC 2025 – JUN 2026

The Ballad of GarCo

No single character threads General chat more thoroughly than GarCo, and he does it without ever being a main character anywhere else. Through October, November, and December 2025 he's just a normal, visibly sharp trader — asking about production costs, helping a game-dev hobbyist debug material-tag parsing at 1 a.m., quietly running what people later remember as "space trucking." Then, in the last week of December, he vanishes. "GarCo's been gone for a bit too," someone notes on January 2. By January 10 the questions start: "Damn who quit? GarCo?" "Pirates abducted GarCo??" Nobody seems to actually know why; he just stopped logging in, mid-economy, the way people do.

What happens next is the clearest evidence in this entire archive that a server can grow its own folklore in real time. Within a month GarCo is a benchmark ("replicate GarCo's starter hauler, and saw that in half"), a saint people pray to for market relief ("Pray to the lord market crasher GarCo"), and a cautionary myth with invented lore nobody bothers fact-checking: by April 15, 2026, players are performing a bit where one asks to be told "the tale of GarCo," another claims to "faintly remember the taste of GarCo's oxygen against my lips," and a third adds, entirely straight-faced, that GarCo "also created his very own GarCoin to scam new players with" — a scam that, as far as this log shows, never actually happened.

I will never forgive GarCoa player, June 2026, about a market he hasn't touched in half a year

He is not dead. He did not do anything. The galaxy just needed a myth and he happened to be standing there when it started looking.

CH. 04MID DEC 2025

The Ugly Week

Not everything the public channel produced was good-natured. In mid-December 2025, after a run of suspiciously fast top-leaderboard climbs, a chunk of chat spends an uncomfortable afternoon generalizing about which nationality is more likely to cheat, backed by someone pasting anti-cheat industry PDFs as evidence. It does not reflect well on General, and to the chat's partial credit, the pushback is immediate and comes from inside the accusation itself — a player sarcastically thanks the room for not accusing him of cheating despite his own high rank, and a Chinese player directly challenges the logic, pointing out it took him a month of honest play to reach the same numbers being treated as proof of guilt. The thread fades within the day. It's included here not for color but because it happened, in public, in the same chat that produced the chicken joke and the GarCo myth — this is what a server actually sounds like at scale, not just its best lines.

CH. 05NOV 2025 – SEP 2026

Coss, Before Coss

If you already know how this game's trade guilds turn out, General chat hides one more gut-punch in plain sight. On November 2, 2025, a brand-new player tagged [TiT] Coss shows up asking the most basic questions in the log — why his materials show 0/2, whether to prioritize buildings or research, whether tech choices are permanent. He's eager, a little lost, very online ("RIP," he types, after his first production run goes net negative). By February 2026 the same Coss is publicly diagnosing the entire server's mood:

I can see why everyone is quitting the game. It literally feels like a jobCoss, February 2026

followed, days later, by pure dark comedy — "Everyone says 'Oh, I'm burnt out'… I don't want to hear it, make me more metal bars." He is joking about burnout while, eighteen months of game-time to the day, he is already standing on the beginning of his own cliff edge. By September 2026, in a different chat entirely, Coss will dismantle every base he owns and quit for real, in what F4T's own archive calls the Cosspocalypse. General chat is the only place that shows you the whole arc: the new player, the joke, the warning, and — elsewhere, later — the fall. He told everyone exactly how it would feel, seven months before it happened to him.

CH. 062026

The Plateau

Growth doesn't continue forever. By April 2026, players are openly debating whether the active population is flat or shrinking; by autumn the burnout language that started as a Coss bit has become the room's default register, same as it did privately inside TiT and F4T and GP.
